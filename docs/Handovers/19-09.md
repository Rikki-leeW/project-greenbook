## 3. Product identity

The application is called **Garden of Mine**.

Use **GOM** when an abbreviation is needed.

Do not call it “My Own Garden” or “MOG.” Those names were used accidentally during development and have now been corrected conceptually.

**Sprig** is the garden helper within Garden of Mine. Sprig must remain a distinct character and identity. Garden of Mine is the place and product; Sprig is the observant keeper who helps the gardener notice and remember.

Sprig’s role is captured by:

> You grow the garden. Sprig keeps the story.

## 4. Product philosophy

Garden of Mine is a personal botanical chronicle, not an accounting system, farm-management dashboard or generic plant database.

It should help a gardener:

- Remember what was planted and where.
- Record the history of individual plants and growing stories.
- Notice changes over time.
- Compare real experiences from their own garden.
- Preserve photographs, notes, harvests, trials and useful knowledge.
- Understand relationships among plants, places, products, ingredients and outcomes.
- Feel that their garden’s story is being gently kept.

The application should feel:

- Warm
- Personal
- Calm
- Botanical
- Tactile
- Thoughtful
- Whimsical without becoming childish
- Clear enough to support substantial garden records

Sprig should support the gardener through memory, observation and questions. Sprig should not pretend to know facts that the gardener has not recorded.

## 8. Intelligence layer direction

Rikki wants Sprig to feel more alive.

The intelligence layer should be introduced gradually and remain trustworthy. It should begin by making better use of Garden of Mine’s existing structured records before adding broad generative behaviour.

Useful early directions include:

- Questions about individual plants and their histories
- Finding related notes, photographs, harvests, trials, products and growing conditions
- Comparing similar growing stories
- Explaining what evidence supports an observation
- Suggesting questions the gardener may want to explore
- Surfacing missing information gently
- Turning Sprig’s existing notices into conversational starting points

Sprig should avoid pretending that a single event is a proven pattern. Language should reflect the amount and quality of recorded evidence.

Future intelligence work should preserve privacy and the local-first character of Garden of Mine. Any external AI connection must be introduced deliberately, with clear boundaries around what garden data leaves the device.

## 9. Visual direction

The next major product phase is visual development.

The application currently works well but still feels too much like an accounting or record-management application. Rikki wants it to feel like entering Sprig’s living garden world.

The agreed order is:

1. Finalize Sprig’s character design.
2. Create a repeatable Sprig reference sheet.
3. Define a restrained visual system from that world.
4. Redesign **Plants** first because it is the most important and frequently used section.
5. Review the result before extending the design across the rest of the application.
6. Keep the interface readable and functional while introducing warmth and visual storytelling.

Avoid covering every screen with heavy illustration. Use artwork as atmosphere, orientation, emotional punctuation and empty-state support.

## 10. Sprig character direction

Sprig is:

- A garden goblin/helper
- The Garden Keeper
- Warm, capable and observant
- Knowledgeable without seeming old
- Cute without looking like a toddler
- Whimsical without looking childish
- Playful but trustworthy for adults
- Clearly non-human
- Kind and calm rather than manic or mischievous
- A keeper of records, photographs and garden stories

Physical direction:

- Peach or warm natural skin, never green
- Rosy cheeks and nose
- Light freckles
- Large warm chestnut eyes
- Expressive but soft eyebrows
- Small rounded goblin jaw
- Pointed ears
- Compact goblin proportions
- Chestnut, slightly tousled hair
- Leaves and moss integrated naturally into the hair or clothing
- Rustic handmade clothes
- Satchel or notebook
- Barely visible expression lines suggesting experience
- No heavy ageing
- No human square jaw
- No severe adult eyebrows
- No oversized hat that makes him look like a human or hobbit in costume
- No frightening, uncanny or wizened appearance

Desired illustration style:

- Storybook watercolor and gouache
- Fine botanical detail
- Warm parchment and woodland palette
- Soft, tactile materials
- Adult-facing editorial quality
- Consistent enough to build a reusable character system
