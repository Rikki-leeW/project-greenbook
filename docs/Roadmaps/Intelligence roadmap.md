Sprig Intelligence V2
1. The purpose

Sprig Intelligence exists to turn a gardener's accumulated records into personal garden knowledge.

It should increasingly answer:

Is this normal for my garden?
Have I seen this before?
What usually happens next?
What's different this time?
What worked better before?
Is there a pattern I haven't noticed?
What did I do last time?

The distinction is important.

Sprig isn't primarily trying to know gardening.

Sprig is trying to know your garden.

External horticultural knowledge can eventually complement that memory, but it must never masquerade as something learned from the gardener's own history.

2. The fundamental architecture

This becomes the backbone:

GARDEN RECORDS
The truth the gardener actually recorded
        ↓
EVIDENCE
The relevant pieces of those records
        ↓
DERIVED CONTEXT
What Sprig can calculate or resolve
        ↓
OBSERVATIONS
What the evidence appears to describe
        ↓
PATTERNS
Relationships across multiple observations/records
        ↓
INSIGHTS
Useful things Sprig can responsibly say
        ↓
SPRIG SMART
Where those insights meet the gardener

That middle matters enormously.

Current Intelligence V1 can often go directly:

record → calculation → insight

We retain that for things Sprig can calculate directly.

V2 adds the intermediate layers needed for language, recurrence, environmental associations and eventually photographs.

3. Records remain garden truth

This is binding.

Plant Stories, Journal entries, Harvests, photographs, Growing Places, Growing Recipes, Products, Trials, Plans and other genuine Sprig records remain the source of truth.

An AI interpretation does not rewrite:

“This stupid potato is floppy again.”

into:

“Potato suffered water stress.”

The original sentence survives untouched.

Sprig may separately derive:

candidate observation: wilting

But the gardener's record remains exactly what they wrote.

This extends the philosophy already present in Sprig:

Sprig Intelligence is derived. It does not own garden truth.

The existing recipe architecture already preserves actual component identities and optional quantities rather than reducing recipes to names.

9. Pattern Engine

This is where Sprig itself becomes powerful.

Observations are individual pieces.

Patterns occur across them.

The deterministic engine should eventually examine at least:

Developmental patterns

Things occurring at similar plant ages.

5 of 8 potato stories began dying back between 14 and 17 weeks.

Seasonal patterns

Things recurring around similar calendar periods.

Looper observations appeared in October in each of the last two years.

Place patterns

Things associated with Growing Places.

Four struggling winter stories were recorded in this location.

Growing Recipe patterns

Different outcomes associated with setup/components.

Stories using these two recipes had noticeably different harvest outcomes.

Variety patterns

Royal Blue has generally continued growing longer than Nadine in your recorded stories.

Intervention patterns

What happened following feeding, moving, treating, pruning, etc.

Three stories improved after being moved.

But Sprig says “improved after”, not “improved because.”

Outcome patterns

Harvest quantity, quality, plant outcome and timing.

Sequence patterns

This is particularly exciting:

yellowing
↓
wilting
↓
dieback
↓
harvest

If similar sequences recur, Sprig may eventually recognise not merely an event but a trajectory.

10. Evidence strength and confidence are different things

We should preserve the useful V1 idea of evidence strength.

But V2 needs to distinguish:

Interpretation confidence

“How confident is Sprig that ‘floppy’ means wilting here?”

from:

Pattern strength

“How much garden history supports this pattern?”

A highly certain observation can still produce a weak pattern if it happened once.

Conversely, twenty records can form a strong pattern even if several individual observations were somewhat ambiguous.

That distinction will protect Sprig from false certainty.

11. Correlation is not causation

Absolutely binding.

Sprig may discover:

Higher-yield potato stories also used PowerFeed.

It cannot leap to:

PowerFeed increased your potato harvest.

Instead:

Your higher-yield potato stories were more likely to include PowerFeed. It may be worth comparing those stories.

Then:

Compare these stories ›

This is where Compare Plants becomes a partner of Intelligence.

Sprig notices the association.

Compare provides the gardener with the evidence.

Garden Trials can eventually test it.

That gives us a lovely progression:

Sprig notices → gardener compares → gardener tests → Sprig learns from Trial.

Garden Trials are particularly valuable because they already preserve question, expectation, changed variable, comparable conditions, observations, outcome and relationships.

12. Trials have higher evidential meaning

A random association and a deliberate Trial should not be treated identically.

If three plants happened to receive Product X and performed well, that's observational evidence.

If a Garden Trial deliberately compared Product X against a control while attempting to keep other conditions comparable, that is stronger for the question the Trial actually tested.

Sprig still shouldn't pretend one home-garden Trial proves universal horticultural causation.

But it can reasonably say:

Your Trial found...

rather than:

I noticed an association...

That's a meaningful distinction.

13. Photographs become first-class evidence

Now: Sprig understands photo date, source/owner, relationships and available human metadata.

Next: text surrounding photographs contributes observations.

Later: visual interpretation can generate candidate observations such as:

flowering

growth-stage change

Your last four photographs appear to show increasing yellowing of the lower leaves over 18 days.

Then Sprig should show the four photographs.

Visual AI must obey the same rule as language AI:

candidate interpretation, not rewritten truth.

Our audit also found that rich photo metadata isn't yet stored consistently by every photo-owning record. For example, current Growing Recipe saving retains photoUrls, but not the richer metadata envelope.

That's a Spring Readiness fix, not a reason to redesign photographs.

Unknown
Sprig genuinely doesn't know.

Sprig calculates what it can prove. AI helps it understand what you meant.

This also means Sprig does not need AI to remain useful.

If AI is unavailable or offline, deterministic Intelligence continues working.

21. Privacy and provider independence

We should not choose an AI provider yet.

The observation architecture must not contain concepts tied to OpenAI, Anthropic, Google or anyone else.

Later, an interpretation request could conceptually be:

evidence → interpreter → candidate observations

The interpreter might eventually be:

hosted AI
local model
on-device capability
another future technology

Sprig doesn't care.

Also, because the current application is a static PWA, we do not put a secret commercial API key into browser JavaScript.

Provider/infrastructure comes later.

22. Storage strategy

We don't need to rip apart Dexie.

The current GardenData model already aggregates Sprig's record collections.

The first Intelligence structures can therefore eventually be added to GardenData and normalised for older gardens.

Later database evolution can split large collections into dedicated IndexedDB tables if scale warrants it.

Architecture first. Database optimisation later.

23. Don't store everything forever just because AI said it

This is important.

We should distinguish:

Ephemeral derivation
Cheap things Sprig can recalculate whenever needed.

from:

Persisted interpretation
Things expensive or valuable to retain, such as an AI interpretation, gardener correction or learned vocabulary association.

from:

Garden truth
Actual gardener-created records.

Otherwise Intelligence will bloat the garden snapshot with mountains of disposable machine output.

We should persist only what gives durable value.

25. Spring 2026 Readiness comes before clever AI

This is our immediate engineering priority.

Before you start pouring a season's worth of real gardening into Sprig, we fix the places where valuable future evidence would otherwise be permanently lost.

From the audit, I currently see four important readiness items:

Real Product relationships in Journal/interventions, while preserving free-text compatibility.
Rich photo metadata consistency across record types where it is currently only saving URLs/dates.
Temporal-context integrity, particularly making sure Growing History remains reliable and allowing Intelligence to distinguish exact history from assumptions.
Evidence-friendly provenance, making sure relationships necessary to reconstruct context aren't flattened or discarded.

Importantly, we do not add twenty new form questions.

Our binding rule is:

Derive context wherever Sprig already knows it. Ask the gardener only when Sprig genuinely doesn't know.
